How to label

Every photo here is a sticky liner from a moth trap. Your boxes and labels teach the trap's camera to find and name the moths on its own, so a grower knows when to spray. Careful beats fast: one wrong label teaches the model something wrong, and a moth you skip teaches it to miss moths.

Your first photo, step by step

  1. Pick a photo under To do in the list on the left (on a phone: ☰).
  2. The models have already guessed. Their boxes are dashed: nobody has checked them yet. Your job is to check every one, and to add any insect they missed.
  3. Press Tab (or ▶) to jump to the next box to check. The photo zooms to it and the box is outlined.
  4. Look at it. If the guess is right, press Enter (or ✓ Right). If it's wrong, press the right number (or tap the right card). Either way you move on, and the box turns solid: checked.
  5. If a box is on nothing, or off the liner, press Delete.
  6. Scan the whole liner once more for insects with no box, and draw one round each (Boxes).
  7. When the counter says every box is checked, press Done with this photo. A reviewer looks at it next.

Work is saved as you go (the green Saved at the bottom of the photo). If it ever turns red, don't close the tab: it keeps trying, and tells you when it has saved.

The screen

  • Left: your photos. The numbers are boxes checked / boxes on the photo. \ or the button at the top left of the photo hides the list to give the photo more room.
  • Middle: the photo. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to move around, F to fit the whole liner again.
  • Right: the selected box close up, what the models thought of it, the label cards, and settings. Show can list only the boxes nobody has checked, or the ones the model was least sure of first.
  • Your name at the top left opens the menu: this guide, your panel widths, log out.

Pass 1: what is it?

Pass 1 is for everyone. Each box gets one of three labels:

KeyLabelWhat goes here
1mothAny moth: whole, worn bald, flattened, wings spread or folded, half under another insect. If you can see moth wings (scaly, often patterned, held flat or like a tent) it's a moth.
2other insectAnything else that was alive: flies, gnats, wasps, bees, beetles, lacewings, leafhoppers, spiders, earwigs. Flies have two clear wings with veins; wasps have a narrow waist; beetles have hard shiny wing cases.
3debrisOn the liner but not a whole insect: loose legs, a wing on its own, scale smudges, leaf and bark bits, seeds, hair, glue blobs, droppings, dirt.
Delete(no label)Only for a box on nothing at all, or on something off the liner (the trap's frame, the table, a finger).
  • Keep debris boxes; don't delete them. The trap's camera sees debris too, and it has to learn that it isn't a moth.
  • Part of a moth is still a moth if most of the body is there. A single wing or a lone leg is debris.
  • Unsure between moth and other insect? Label your best guess and press U (unsure). Don't skip it.

Boxes

Box every insect on the liner: tiny, blurred, half hidden, overlapping, decayed ones too. A model trained on photos where small ones were left out learns to miss them, and its counts drift towards the big insects.

  • Tight: the box touches the insect's outermost edges, wings and all, with a hair of space. Not the antennae or legs sticking far out, and not its shadow or the glue around it.
  • One insect per box. If a box covers two, shrink it to one and draw the other.
  • Overlapping insects each get their own box, even if the boxes overlap. The site tags them for you (O).
  • Draw a box: press B (or Draw box) and drag across the insect. Or, in draw mode, just click the insect and the site draws a box round it for you; check it fits.
  • Fix a box: select it and drag its edges or corners, or drag the middle to move it. L locks a box so it can't move by accident; checked boxes can't be dragged unless you turn that off under Show.
  • Undo anything with ⌘Z (redo ⇧⌘Z), or the arrows.

Tags on a box

Ecut offThe insect runs off the edge of the photo.
WwornDecayed, rubbed bald, squashed, or mostly scales gone: hard to judge from its looks.
UunsureYou labelled it but aren't confident. Use it freely: an honest "unsure" is worth more than a guess.
OoverlappingAdded by itself when two boxes share part of their area.

Pass 2: which species?

Switch to 2 · species on the right. Tab now jumps to the moths that have no species yet. Anyone can do pass 2, but only name a species when you're sure from what you can see: each person's species calls are scored on hidden test photos, so the team knows whose calls to trust.

When in doubt, press 0 (moth, unsure). A wrong species is worse than none: the trap counts the three pests to time sprays, and a wrong name moves those counts.

  1. Size first. The liner's grid squares are a known size, and the details on the right show the box's length in mm. Most mix-ups are fixed by size alone.
  2. Shape at rest. Leafrollers (OBLR, RBLR and other tortricids) fold their wings into a broad bell with square shoulders. CM, OFM and LAW are narrower, with the wings folded back like a long roof.
  3. Marks. Then look for the species' own marks below. On glue, wings are often spread and the scales rubbed off, so marks can be faint or missing: that's what 0 is for.

The species

Sizes are rough wingspans, wings spread, which is how most end up on the glue. Folded, a moth is about half as long.

1 CM · codling moth

About 15–22 mm. Grey, with fine wavy cross-lines like wood grain. The giveaway is a copper or bronze shiny patch at the tip of each forewing, edged darker. The main apple pest. Lure: codling moth.

2 OFM · oriental fruit moth

Small: about 10–15 mm. Dark grey-brown, narrow, with a row of short pale dashes along the front edge of the forewing and faint pale bands. No copper patch, which tells it from a small CM.

3 OBLR · obliquebanded leafroller

Larger: about 20–30 mm. A tan to light brown bell with square shoulders and dark brown bands: a patch at the wing base, a broad band running slantwise across the middle (the "oblique band"), and a spot near the tip.

4 RBLR · redbanded leafroller (look-alike)

About 13–17 mm, smaller than OBLR. A pale brown bell with one narrow reddish-brown band running slantwise across the wings, and no dark patch at the base. Not a pest we count, but easy to call OBLR by mistake.

5 LAW · lesser appleworm (look-alike)

The smallest: about 9–11 mm. Dark brown with grey and orange-brown scales and fine pale bands. It looks a lot like OFM and turns up in OFM traps. If you can't split it from OFM, use 0.

6 other tortricid

A leafroller-type moth (the bell shape) that is none of the above.

7 other moth

Any moth that isn't a tortricid: noctuids (stout, often bigger), pyralids and crambids (long and thin, snout-like), geometrids (broad thin wings), tiny micro-moths. Most bycatch ends up here.

0 moth?, species unsure

A moth you can't name with confidence. Always better than a guess.

Problem photos

Under This photo (on a phone, More settings):

  • Unusable: too blurred, dark or glary to label at all. Tick it and press Done.
  • Wrong scale: the mm sizes are clearly off (a moth reads as 2 mm or 80 mm). Keep labelling; it just tells us not to trust the sizes.
  • Not a gridded liner: it isn't a sticky liner with a grid at all.

Comments and review

  • Right-click anywhere on the photo (on a computer) to pin a comment: a question for a reviewer, or a note.
  • A reviewer either accepts your photo or sends it back with comments pinned where something needs fixing. Sent-back photos come first in your list. Fix what each comment says, then press Resolve on it, then Done again.
  • Two-person photos: some photos are labelled by two people separately. Where you disagreed, a third person decides each box marked with a comment.
  • Reference photos are labelled by the team's most careful people; everyone else is scored against them.

Hidden test photos

A few photos in your list are hidden tests: they look like any other photo and already have reference labels. Label them as you would any photo. The dashboard scores them: found (how many of the reference's insects you boxed), right (how many of those got the reference's label), kept wrong guess (how often you pressed Enter on a wrong model guess without looking) and species right.

Common mistakes

  • Pressing Enter without looking. The models are wrong often enough that the test photos measure this.
  • Deleting debris. Label it debris instead.
  • Skipping tiny or blurred insects. Box them; tag U if you can't tell what they are.
  • Loose boxes with lots of glue around the insect, or one box round two insects.
  • Guessing species. Use 0 when you aren't sure.
  • Pressing Done with boxes left unchecked. The site warns you and outlines them in red.
